## Scientific audience and possible relevance

The results concern one explicitly defined **infinite Ammann–Beenker graph**, with two distinct onsite models: the original confined Hamiltonian and the bounded plateau Hamiltonian (§4.112). Their value is not that dark states, localized waves or single-edge perturbations are new ideas, but that several spectral statements have been proved for the complete infinite operator rather than inferred from a finite sample.

- **Mathematicians studying spectral theory and discrete Schrödinger operators** may be interested in the exact two-dimensional crossing eigenspace, the certified real spectral gap, and the response to a rank-one edge change. The signed single-edge theorem (§4.97) identifies the two nearby simple eigenvalues, the direction of the moving level, a quadratic energy-error bound and a bound on the moving eigenvector's exterior component. For the different coordinated controls, §§4.107–4.110 identify the two exact plane energies and establish a quantitative separation from the complete exterior real spectrum under explicit sufficient control bounds.
- **Researchers on quasicrystals, compact localized states and flat bands** may find the contrast between two interventions useful. Changing the first-ring parameter shifts one compact mode's energy exactly while preserving both mode shapes. Changing the connecting edge instead protects the dark combination but gives the bright-derived state a controlled coupling to the rest of the infinite network. The original localized patterns themselves were known before this formalization. The coordinated matching (§§4.99–4.100) supplies a distinct bounded symmetric intervention that preserves the whole two-mode plane exactly.
- **Theorists working on wave control, photonic lattices or coupled-resonator networks** may find the temporary-edge results relevant as a mathematical starting point. The proved duration-independent leakage estimates require supplied norm-preserving, domain-preserving evolutions that intertwine their respective Hamiltonians. These conditional single-edge bounds do not establish a general physical propagator or the L6 preparation theorem. The distinct coordinated-control theorem (§4.105) constructs three unit-duration pulses preparing any normalized target from dark up to a common phase, with exact zero leakage throughout. The centered spectral theorem (§4.110) additionally guarantees static isolation for sufficiently weak ideal controls. The subsequent theorem (§4.111) establishes exact preparation with arbitrarily weak controls and a prescribed fraction of the certified baseline gap through longer pulses. This is a mathematical result for ideal coordination, not an experimental or noise-robustness claim.
- **Researchers on localized spectral defects** may be interested in the bounded plateau theorem (§4.112): exact double eigenvalue and full real spectral gap at least 1/122 without relying on compact resolvent, plus a finite-rank comparison with a uniform reference bulk. Neither the precise essential spectrum nor multi-pair control has been established.
- **Researchers in formalized mathematics** may be interested in how natural domains of unbounded operators, complete real eigenbases, explicit spectral gaps and quantitative perturbation estimates are handled in Lean 4 and Mathlib.

These are **plausible research audiences**, not evidence of originality, practical usefulness or scientific impact. Independent expert evaluation and comparison with the specialist literature are still needed.
